Two things or three?

Adjectives change when we compare.

Comparative two things

Compares two people, animals, objects or situations.

Sara is taller than Noor.

Superlative three or more

Names the highest or lowest degree in a group of three or more, or in a defined group.

Sara is the tallest.

Ali > Ahmed

Adjective: FAST

Count the beats

A syllable is one beat of sound: fast (1), hap-py (2), beau-ti-ful (3). Syllables are a useful guide, not an absolute rule. Some two-syllable adjectives allow both forms: cleverer or more clever, narrower or more narrow. Usage can vary.

Irregular forms: learn them one by one
good→better→best
bad→worse→worst
far→farther / further→farthest / furthest

Far: farther is common for physical distance; further is also used for distance and is the usual choice for "more" or "additional" (further information). Context decides.

The rules you now own

Spelling notes: adjectives ending in -e only add -r / -st (nice, nicer, nicest). Use less / least for the opposite meaning (less tall, the least tall). Some two-syllable adjectives allow both forms. When unsure, check a dictionary.
